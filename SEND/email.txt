Subject: Tests caught 6 of 25 faults. Is that known?


Dear Professor ——,

I'm an independent developer with no university affiliation. I have a
small dataset I can't evaluate alone.

Building a scientific codebase under the rule that every result carries
a check that would catch it being wrong, I ended up recording 25 faults,
each annotated with the mechanism that caught it:

    deriving something previously asserted        7
    faults in the verification machinery itself   7
    an automated check fired                      6
    comparison against an external known value    4
    cross-referencing an earlier release          1

Tests caught 6 of 25. The largest category needed someone to re-derive
a quantity that had been typed in — work no test performs, because the
code was self-consistent and everything passed. And the checking
apparatus failed at about the rate of the code it checks, 7 against 18.

The data exists because of a convention, not a tool: when a check's
premise turns out false, it is flipped and kept with its history rather
than deleted. Deleting is normal, and would have erased nine data
points.

Is a fault corpus annotated by catching-mechanism useful to anyone, or
does this already exist? Defects4J and BugSwarm are mined from version
control, which gives the fix but rarely what surfaced it.

Corpus and a six-page write-up attached; the corpus is the part worth
your time. Happy to be told it's a solved problem.
