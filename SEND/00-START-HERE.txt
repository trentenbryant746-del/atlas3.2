SEND — everything needed for the professor email
=================================================

DO THIS
-------
1. Open paper.html and fault-corpus.html in a browser.
   Press Cmd+P, choose "Save as PDF". Two files, done.
   (No PDF tool was installed, so they are print-ready HTML.)

2. Copy the body of email.txt. Replace "Professor ——" with a name.

3. Attach BOTH PDFs. The corpus is the artifact; the paper argues
   about it. If you attach only one, attach the corpus.

4. Send to ONE person first. Wait a week before the next.


WHAT EACH FILE IS
-----------------
email.txt          210 words, copy-paste ready. The short version.
email-long.txt     350 words, if you want more context up front.
paper.html         Six pages. Print to PDF.
fault-corpus.html  The 25 faults. Print to PDF. THIS IS THE ARTIFACT.

THE ONE-LINE VERSION
--------------------
"I recorded 25 faults from a physics codebase with the mechanism that
caught each one. The test code failed about as often as the code it
tested. Is that known?"


WHY THE EMAIL SAYS WHAT IT SAYS
-------------------------------
The subject line is the hook: every person who has written test code
has wondered whether their tests are as buggy as their code, and
nobody has a number. That sentence is the reason to open the mail.

The second paragraph says what the software IS -- planetary formation,
nuclear binding, radiative transfer, prebiotic chemistry. An earlier
draft said only "a scientific codebase," which could be anything, and
a reader cannot judge whether 25 faults are representative of anything
without knowing the domain.

"Third iteration of the same system" is accurate and checkable: Atlas
1 and Atlas 2 both exist, Atlas 2 with 51 engine modules against this
one's 107. It conveys that the work has history WITHOUT claiming a
duration. Do not add one.

The duplicate-constant example is there because one concrete, slightly
funny fault makes the corpus real in a way a table cannot.


BEFORE YOU SEND — three checks
-------------------------------
[ ] The name is right and the person actually works on empirical
    software engineering, software testing, or research software
    engineering. Not a physicist. Not yet.

[ ] You have not mentioned how long the project took. An earlier
    draft said "eight months"; the git history shows two days. Nothing
    in the claim depends on duration.

[ ] You are comfortable being told "this is Bazel" or "n=1." Both are
    fair. The paper concedes both in section 8.
